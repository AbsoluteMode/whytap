# Lil Bro

## Context

The first outside tester found Settings hard to understand. Settings are being
rebuilt around features (2026-11-24-settings-around-features.md). On top of
that the maintainer asked for a helper inside Settings that answers questions
and opens the right place, and approved a mockup and scenario video of
"Lil Bro" after 24 rounds of changes.

## Decision

Lil Bro is one layer over the whole Settings window, built in three steps:

1. **The layer and the mascot, without network (this step).** He lives on the
   "Lil Bro · ask anything" entry at the bottom of the sidebar. A click catches
   him and opens his balloon. Asked where something is, he runs there over the
   window and opens it. Answers come from the feature catalog and tab names on
   this Mac.
2. **Answers from whytap.ai.** A server endpoint in Ask returns text and a plan
   (where to go, what to press), answered by claude-haiku-5-5. This changes the
   "no Whytap servers" invariant, so it has its own explicit switch, privacy
   text and an offline state: 2026-11-24-lil-bro-answers-from-whytap.md.
3. **Actions with confirmation.** Before pressing something that changes a
   setting (download a model, allow a permission, restart) he asks "Press it?",
   says what pressing does, and only after "Yes, press it" runs the existing
   setup action (`FeatureScreenContext.perform`) — the same path as the row's
   button. The "What it needs" rows report their step (`lilBroNeed`), so he
   offers only what the screen itself offers. An offer belongs to one
   feature, requirement and action; right before pressing he checks that
   this feature's screen is still open and the very step is still offered,
   and runs the row's current registration — otherwise nothing is pressed
   ("That step changed, so I didn't press it."). A model row that shows its
   picker ("Choose a model") is never pressed for the person. With several
   required steps he says how many ("3 steps left. First: …"); a permission
   step says macOS asks, or System Settings opens. Feature on/off switches are not
   offered yet: their screens are being rebuilt (#458-#460).

## Why

- **He presses, he does not point.** The maintainer's words: the person should
  remember that this button opens that setting. So he goes to the row a person
  would click — the sidebar row, then the Features row — and punches it.
- **Same calls as the rows, never synthetic clicks.** Each press runs the
  `SettingsWindowSelection` call that row makes (`select`, `open`,
  `showAPIKeys`). Nothing posts events, so permissions, focus and other apps
  are untouched, and a test can prove where he took the person.
- **The window is the map, reported by the views.** Rows report themselves with
  `lilBroSpot`, and every `MacCard` reports itself as a block. He follows every
  screen, including the ones other sessions are building, without those screens
  knowing about him. Cards scrolled out of view are not platforms.
- **Movement from the approved mockup.** Run 340 pt/s, stroll 70 pt/s, arc jump
  up to 90 pt high, ladder above that; from his entry to a high card he drops
  to the window floor beneath it and climbs. Reduce Motion: he appears there.
- **Still while the balloon is open (REV 24).** The mockup shook when he ran
  with an open message. While moving, only a small tag ("On my way…") rides
  with him; the full balloon appears when he stands. Closing it sends him home.
  If his card scrolls away while the balloon is open, he waits at home instead.
- **Smooth over a still window (REV 1, 2026-11-26).** After 2.2.27 (1767)
  the maintainer said the animation lagged, blocks seemed to move, and too
  much text came at once. Three causes, each measured offscreen in
  `LilBroSmoothnessTests` on the real Settings shell:
  - The window observed the controller (`@StateObject`), so every step and
    pose change re-evaluated the whole Settings window: 17 window renders on
    the way to Drop. Now the window only holds him (`@State`); his layer and
    sidebar entry observe him. After: 0 renders for a stroll or a state
    change, 3 on the way to Drop (the two screens he opens).
  - His place was sampled at most 30 times a second; at 340 pt/s that is an
    11 pt step per frame, a stutter on 60–120 Hz displays. Now his place is
    sampled every display refresh while he moves (120 fps offscreen) and the
    clock stops at rest; the control, focus and hover are not rebuilt per
    frame, and his frames keep their own slower clock.
  - The balloon was placed from its size measured on the previous pass, so
    on opening it was drawn at the old size and then moved (155 pt on the way
    to Drop). Now one `Layout` (`LilBroBalloonStage`) measures the balloon and
    the reply field and places them with the tail in the same pass: one place,
    no move. When he opens a feature, the balloon waits up to 0.6 s for the
    screen's setup steps, so "Press it?" is there when it opens instead of
    growing a moment later.
- **A short answer and one action (REV 1).** The balloon shows the answer's
  first paragraph and at most one action: "Press it?", a question to pick,
  Try again with the closest page. The rest (more paragraphs, examples, "I
  opened …", follow-up questions) waits behind **More**, and every new
  question starts short. Earlier questions show their first words.
- **Only the field at first (REV 5).** The maintainer asked that on the first
  request the person sees only the Ask Lil Bro field, and that the (i) icon
  goes. Opened before any question, he shows only the "Ask Lil Bro…"
  field: no greeting, no suggested questions, no disclosure, and the (i)
  button is gone. The balloon comes with the first answer. The whytap.ai
  disclosure (one line, full text under Details) appears by itself only
  after a question this Mac could not answer, and then it is the whole reply
  (REV 7: no "I only know Settings by name", no suggested questions); ⋯ can
  still show it.
- **A one-line greeting (REV 6).** The maintainer suggested a text
  message like "wassup bro", some slang. Over the field,
  one line in his slang ("Wassup bro? What we setting up?"), a different
  one each opening; no buttons, no disclosure. The Send arrow appears only
  once something is typed (its space is kept). With answers on, ⋯ sits next
  to the greeting so they can be turned off before a question.
- **Answers from whytap.ai on by default (REV 8).** The maintainer answered
  "Yes, by default": a question goes straight to whytap.ai with the thinking
  dots, no card on the way. The disclosure card appears only after the person
  turned answers off in ⋯ and the local guide did not understand a question.
  Details: 2026-11-24-lil-bro-answers-from-whytap.md.
- **No scripted scenarios (REV 10, 2026-11-26).** The maintainer: no
  automatic actions, "only help the person on request"; remove the written
  scenarios, and from what the person asks work out whether to open
  something or just answer in the chat. Removed: the local keyword guide
  (`LilBroLocalGuide` and its tests), the "Press it?" setup step he pressed
  after a yes (`LilBroPressable`, `lilBroNeed`), follow-up question buttons
  and the "closest page" offered when whytap.ai could not answer. Every
  question goes to whytap.ai; the model decides whether to answer in the
  balloon or name a place (the server's rules: only when the person asks to
  go, find, set up or change something). With answers off nothing is sent
  and the reply is only the card that turns them on and asks; offline, a
  short message with Try again. A question under three characters is not
  sent. This supersedes the bullets above about "Press it?", the local guide
  and its contract.
- **To the exact row (REV 12, 2026-11-26).** On build 1770 the maintainer
  asked how to set a hotkey so a meeting starts; Lil Bro opened Hotkeys but
  not the Meeting record row ("he showed me the hotkeys but why not record?
  I count the task 50% done"). When the model leads somewhere it now names the
  row: `LilBroRow.catalog` (Hotkeys rows for now, one `row(...)` per line so
  whytap.ai can parse it) and `go: {row, tab}`. He opens the tab, it scrolls
  the row to the middle, he stands on it and outlines it. An unknown row
  falls back to its tab; an older build reads only the tab.
- **Only Sonnet, no switch (REV 13).** The maintainer, on 1772's ⋯ menu:
  "remove this, Lil Bro works only through Sonnet". The answers switch,
  "What is sent to whytap.ai", the Turn on and ask card and the `answersOff`
  reply are gone; every question goes to whytap.ai. ⋯ (in the balloon and
  next to the greeting) keeps Hide the little guy, Clear and Close. Clear
  still ends a pending question and any guiding. Bullets below about
  turning answers off are history.
- **Builder review of #493 (ticket 625).** Turning answers off takes effect
  at once (pending request cancelled, preference rechecked right before
  sending, a late reply and its navigation ignored); Clear ends a pending
  request and leaves him usable; ⋯ → What is sent to whytap.ai shows the
  data-use text while answers are on, with OK and nothing to confirm.
- **Builder review of #493 (ticket 627).** The row outline is derived from
  the led row's current place, not kept as a rectangle: it disappears while
  the row is scrolled away and returns with it, and ends when the focus does
  (another tab, Clear, Turn off answers, a new question, closing). A row that
  cannot be shown, because the person left its tab during the scroll or it
  never reported, outlines nothing (never another card), keeps their tab and
  is not recorded as opened; with the little guy he waits at home.
- **Builder review of #493 (ticket 630).** The person's tab wins for the
  whole row guide: once the sidebar stop opened the tab, the guide only sets
  the row's focus and never selects the tab again, so leaving it while it
  lays out ends the lead. When the row scrolls back, the little guy returns
  to it with the outline while the lead lasts.
- **Builder review of #493 (ticket 631).** A click of the person's own
  while he runs or punches a sidebar row wins: before every press he checks
  that the window is still where his last press left it (tab and feature
  screen); if not, he presses nothing more, outlines nothing, does not claim
  the place and waits at home. The same holds for features' screens.
- **Typing in 1768 lagged because every key press published `draft` on the
  controller**, which the window observed: each letter re-evaluated the
  whole Settings window, his layer, balloon and sprite. No guide search,
  save or request ran per key. Now no key press publishes anything on the
  controller (tested with its `objectWillChange`).
- **Typing and hover touch only what changes (REV 2).** The maintainer:
  "everything lags, from hover to typing in this field". What is typed lives
  in the reply field's own state, so a key press redraws the field only; the
  window no longer observes him, so hover and opening redraw only his layer.
  Measured in `LilBroInteractionTimingTests` through the real field editor,
  three runs each on f430cd28 and on this branch (median ms per change,
  offscreen): hover 1.3–2.7 → 0.8–1.0, a typed character 2.2–5.0 → 1.0–1.6,
  opening the balloon 15–29 → 12–20. About 5 ms of opening is AppKit creating
  and focusing the text field, left as is. Typing now evaluates neither the
  window nor his layer (tested).
- **A cancelled move does nothing.** Closing the balloon or a new question
  cancels the current job, and every wait in it checks before pressing a row,
  moving him or changing his pose, because a late press would navigate behind
  the person's back.
- **He can be hidden.** The ⋯ menu hides the little guy; the sidebar entry stays
  and opens the same balloon, and places open at once.
- **Sprites as data.** A 38-frame sheet (14x16 cells, 8 columns, light and dark)
  with a JSON index, drawn by Gemini 4 Neon from the approved "Hood" pixels. It
  is embedded into the binary, so no packaging step can lose it, and tests read
  the bytes the app draws.

## What was tested

- Route planning: run along a block, one jump to a near block, ladder from
  underneath, drop to the floor then climb, Reduce Motion, scrolled-away cards.
- Balloon placement at the middle, top, right and bottom edges, on a block, and
  from the sidebar entry with the mascot hidden.
- The local guide: people's words for features ("dictate", "meeting notes",
  "island tools"), a hotkey is not an API key, unknown questions get starters.
- Scenarios: caught → balloon; asked about dictation → Drop opened through its
  row, he stands on the screen's first card, a layout pass with the balloon
  open does not move him; from another tab he presses Features first; closing
  sends him home; closing mid-way stops cleanly; hidden or Reduce Motion opens
  at once; offline is sad and changes nothing; the hidden choice persists.
- Offscreen renders of the real Settings shell in light and dark.
- After the builder's review of the first PR: closing the balloon or asking
  again while he punches a row never presses it (every wait in a guide checks
  cancellation before pressing or moving him); opening the balloon from the
  sidebar or by catching him stops a stroll at once; a place is said "opened"
  only once it is open; exact macOS setting and feature names win over broad
  words ("Input Monitoring" is Permissions, "Google voice search" is Google
  search); a macOS setting after "after / once / when / with / if …" in the
  same part of the sentence is context, so the feature opens ("Where is Drop
  after enabling Accessibility?", "Can I enable Drop with my Microphone?");
  otherwise asking to find or allow the setting is Permissions whatever
  feature it is for ("Where do I allow Microphone for Drop?", "For Drop,
  where can I find Microphone?"), and so is a setting named before any
  feature ("Screen Recording for Screenshots") — the builder's 86 direct and
  40 context questions are a test; the setting may be qualified by where
  it lives or who gets it ("open the macOS Accessibility settings", "grant
  Whytap Microphone access", "allow Whytap to use Screen Recording"), and a
  marker that introduces the feature ("With Drop can I enable Microphone?",
  "When using Screenshots can I allow Screen Recording?") does not make the
  setting context: a marker holds until the main question starts ("can I",
  "where", "how"), not for a fixed number of words; every mention of a
  setting counts and a verb counts only for the mention it takes; "turn
  Microphone on" and "give Drop access to the Microphone" are requests when
  the person does them, not "Does Drop turn the Microphone on?"; "without
  having to enable" is context; "show me" and "tell me" start the main
  question; a negated verb ("I can't enable Microphone access") explains
  rather than asks; "give Drop Microphone access", "turn the Microphone off"
  and "disable" are requests — the builder's second matrix (ticket 585), its
  context controls and three independent reviews' cases are 531 more test
  questions; "turn on / switch on / give" count when I, we, you or please
  do them or as an instruction after a comma or sentence ("For Drop, turn
  on Microphone.") — the ticket-590 matrix is 798 more; inside a question
  about what something else does ("Does Drop, by default, turn on
  Microphone?", also "Doesn't Drop …", "Can't Drop …", or across a line
  break, after a lead-in like "On this Mac," or embedded as "whether Drop
  can …") an instruction after a comma is that thing's action when a part of
  the sentence before it asks what the feature, it or Whytap does; "Don't
  open Drop, turn on Microphone." (a verb after "don't"), a statement before
  the comma ("Drop is installed, turn on Microphone.") or an ended sentence
  ("Does Drop work? Turn on Microphone.") leaves an instruction; typographic
  apostrophes read as plain ones — with the ticket-594, 597 and 600
  boundaries, 3150 in that test; this is a keyword guide, not a parser, so the tests are the
  contract; known limits: a comma inside the leading part ("if I restart,
  then enable Microphone") or a quoted macOS prompt inside the question is
  still read as a request; "in case" is a phrase, not the Case
  feature; his own controls show hover,
  press and a focus ring, and the mascot is a control only while a click
  catches him; with Reduce Motion his frames hold still.
- Step 3: he offers the first required step, not an optional one; presses
  only after yes and runs to that row; "I'll do it", closing and a step done
  meanwhile press nothing; tabs never offer a press; hidden he presses at
  once after yes; on a fresh Mac the real Drop "What it needs" row reports
  Download free model and he asks before pressing (render).
- After the builder's review of the step-3 PR (ticket 609): a row whose
  action changed before yes, a step done while he runs to it (animated and
  immediate), leaving the feature (even for an identical row on another
  feature, before or during the run) and rows still from the last screen
  never press anything; an unchanged step runs once through its current
  registration; a model picker row is not offered; the copy counts steps.

- REV 2–4: hover, opening and typing timed through the real field editor
  (numbers above);
  the one-line disclosure keeps its full text under Details; Turn on and ask
  sends only the question this Mac did not understand.
- REV 1 (smoothness): the window is not re-rendered by a stroll or a state
  change; the balloon is placed once when it opens at Drop, with its step
  already offered; the greeting's link shows the disclosure and never turns
  answers on; More resets with every question and on close. Before/after on

## Contract for the local guide (removed in REV 10)

Removed on 2026-11-26 with the guide itself; kept as history.


`LilBroLocalGuide` is an offline keyword guide, not a parser of English. It
names a destination from the feature catalog, tab names and the macOS setting
names; it cannot read every way a sentence can be built, and each new rule
for one phrasing can move another.

- **Acceptance is its tests.** The routing contract is
  `LilBroLocalGuideTests` in
  `Tests/SidekeyTests/Settings/LilBro/LilBroModelTests.swift`: about 3,900
  routed questions, including the builder's fixtures for tickets 581
  (126), 585 (504), 590, 594, 597 and 600 (3,150 in one test), and the
  cases from three independent reviews.
- **What blocks a PR:** a failure of those tests, or an app defect (wrong
  navigation, a press without Yes, a crash, broken cancellation, layout,
  accessibility).
- **What does not block:** a new phrasing that the guide answers wrongly.
  It is added to these tests as its own task, with the expected destination,
  and fixed there; the PR is not returned for it.

Why: PR #464 was blocked six times in a row (tickets 581, 585, 590, 594, 597,
600), each time on new phrasings, and each narrow fix opened the next one. A
wrong answer costs the person one more question in the balloon; it never
presses anything or changes a setting. Agreed with the orchestrator on
2026-11-26.

## Rejected

- Synthetic clicks or accessibility presses on the controls: fragile, touch the
  person's focus and look like automation.
- A separate chat panel or a chat card in the corner (variants A and B).
- Running while the balloon is open.
- Placing the balloon from a size measured on the previous pass (it moved
  after appearing), and a 30 fps cap on his movement (REV 1).
- Shipping the sheet as a bundle resource copied by the packaging scripts.
- An orb that walks, a "pretty" helper, a pixel anime girl, boys 1 and 2.
